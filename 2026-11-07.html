Surrogate-guided agentic search finds ground-state orderings of disordered crystals

arXiv · statistical mechanics · 6/10

The abstract reports that the surrogate-guided search found the global-minimum ordering in 94% of runs, versus 55% for random walk, Monte Carlo and genetic-algorithm baselines. Benchmarks covered 14 cases with exhaustively enumerated ground truth, and the method recovered the reported ordering for Na2/3FePO4. Disordered crystals, where atoms share sites at partial occupancy, must be assigned a specific arrangement before many simulations can run, so faster ordering searches are practically useful.

Spin-orbit anisotropy does not destabilize Dirac spin liquid in Yb delafossites

arXiv · ML materials discovery · 7/10

The abstract reports that XXZ and bond-dependent couplings generate no relevant Lorentz-scalar operator within a single layer, and that easy-plane anisotropy at the 120° transition selects the chiral-Ising class, based on exact diagonalization. It is a theory paper with no materials screening. The symmetry arguments show how bond structure constrains which interactions could destabilize a Dirac spin liquid.

Ising Ampère spin liquid shows hidden entropic charge order

Triangular Ising antiferromagnet: weak-coupling selection and finite-torus entropy bounds

arXiv · spin models & criticality · 6/10

The abstract proves that on odd L×L tori, selecting ground states with a second-neighbor interaction gives a minimum interface length of 4L and reduces the exponential ground-state degeneracy to polynomial growth. It also gives explicit entropy-density bounds, with errors scaling as min(M,N) to the power −2. The work is exact combinatorics rather than Monte Carlo simulation.

Long-range quantum Ising glass: spin-glass order after transverse-field quench

arXiv · spin models & criticality · 6/10

The abstract reports that Edwards-Anderson spin-glass order after a transverse-field quench can overshoot or undershoot its endpoint values, depending on the initial shell. It also suggests that weak dephasing could serve as a noise probe for quantum simulators. It is a theory result with no comparison to a real material.

Rubidium spin oscillator retrodicts past measurements below the Heisenberg bound

arXiv · quantum physics · 6/10

The uncertainty relation limits how precisely non-commuting quantities can be predicted, but not how well past outcomes can be inferred. By combining weak continuous measurements before and after a time t, the experiment retrodicts position-like and momentum-like spin quadratures about 1.01 dB below the bound for 1.4 hours, using 5.17×10^10 rubidium atoms. The gain is small, but it shows that measurement information, not only the quantum state, sets how well we can know the past.
